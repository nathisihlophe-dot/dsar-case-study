# DSAR, Full Case

Setting: a mid-sized financial-services firm (retail banking and lending).
The request: "I want a copy of all the personal data you hold about me."
Requester (fictional): an existing customer, referred to here as the data subject.

## Step 1, Intake and logging

The request arrived by email to customer services and was forwarded to the privacy team. It was logged with a reference number and the date received, starting the one-month deadline under Article 12(3). The channel, the requester's stated identity, and what they asked for were recorded, and receipt was acknowledged back to the requester.

The request did not mention "DSAR" or the GDPR, it simply said "send me my data," and was logged as an access request on that basis.

The deadline is one month from receipt, extendable by two more months for complex or numerous requests, provided the requester is told about the extension and the reason for it within the first month.

## Step 2, Identity verification

Identity was confirmed through the customer's existing authenticated banking channel rather than by requesting new documentation, since the firm already holds sufficient information to verify a known customer this way.

The one-month clock was not paused, since verification did not require anything beyond what was already available.

## Step 3, Locating the data

The customer's data was located across the following systems:

| System | Likely personal data |
|--------|----------------------|
| Core banking platform | Account details, transactions, balances |
| Lending / credit system | Applications, credit decisions, repayment history |
| CRM | Contact details, service notes, relationship history |
| Marketing platform | Preferences, consents, campaign history |
| Call-centre / recordings | Call logs, and possibly recorded calls |
| Email / correspondence | Letters and emails to and from the customer |

A data map was used to search each system, including unstructured records such as emails, call recordings, and notes. Results were assembled in one place for review.

## Step 4, Exemptions and third-party data

Each located item was reviewed individually before disclosure.

One call note referenced another customer by name. That reference was redacted, since the right of access does not extend to another person's personal data (Article 15(4)).

No material in this case fell under legal privilege or fraud-prevention exemptions. Each redaction was recorded along with the reason for it.

## Step 5, Response package

The response included: a copy of the personal data itself, the purposes it is processed for, the categories of data involved, who it is shared with, how long it is kept, the customer's rights (correction, deletion, restriction, objection, and the right to complain to the supervisory authority), and a note on the automated decision-making used in the lending system, with a plain explanation of its logic, since Article 15 requires this where automated decisions are involved.

The data was provided in a commonly used electronic format, delivered through the authenticated banking channel, with a covering note:

"Enclosed is a copy of the personal data we hold about you, along with information about how and why we use it and the rights available to you. Some information has been removed where it relates to other people."

## Step 6, Audit trail and close

The following was recorded for accountability under Article 5(2): the date received and the date responded, how identity was verified, which systems were searched, the exemption applied to the redacted call note and the reason, and what was sent and how.

The request was then closed on the register, with the record kept as evidence.

## Notes on this case

The identity check here was straightforward, since the customer was already authenticated through an existing channel. A request without that channel available would need a different verification approach.

The main redaction involved a single third-party reference in a call note. No automated decision-making objection was raised by the customer, so the response included the required explanation without further action needed.
