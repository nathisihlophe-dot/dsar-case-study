# Data Subject Access Request (DSAR), Worked Case Study

**A privacy-operations portfolio project.** It walks a single Data Subject Access Request through its full lifecycle at a financial-services firm: intake, identity verification, locating data across systems, applying exemptions, preparing the response, and keeping the audit trail.

> **Note on data.** This is an illustrative portfolio exercise using a fictional requester and a representative financial-services context. It contains no real personal data. It is designed to align with GDPR principles; it is not legal advice or a compliance determination.

> **Jurisdiction.** EU GDPR, modelled in a Finnish context. The supervisory authority referenced is the Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman (*Tietosuojavaltuutetun toimisto*).

---

## Why this project

Handling access requests is one of the most common day-to-day tasks in privacy operations, and one of the most revealing: the deadline is rarely the hard part; finding the data across scattered systems, applying exemptions correctly, and keeping a defensible record are. This case study shows that end-to-end operational competence on a single realistic request.

## The four questions this project answers

**1. What is the problem?**
A customer of a financial-services firm submits a request to access all personal data the firm holds about them. Under the GDPR the firm must respond within a month, provide the data and the required accompanying information, and do so without disclosing other people's data or protected material. The data is spread across banking, CRM, marketing, and call-centre systems.

**2. What did I produce?**
A complete DSAR handling record: the intake log, identity-verification step, a data-location map across systems, an exemptions analysis, a drafted response package, and the audit trail, plus a short reflection on where these requests usually go wrong.

**3. How does it align with GDPR?**
The workflow maps to Articles 12 (transparency, timeframe, and the modalities of responding) and 15 (the right of access and what must be provided), with exemptions considered against the rights-of-others and legal-privilege limits.

**4. What business value does it provide?**
A repeatable, defensible DSAR process protects the firm from complaints and regulatory findings, builds customer trust, and turns an ad-hoc scramble into a controlled operation with an evidence trail.
